# Epic 8 Retrospective: Lead QA Manual Review Workflow

**Date:** 2026-02-04
**Project:** Dark Software Factory
**Facilitator:** Scrum Master
**Reviewer:** AI Assistant

## 1. Epic Overview & Context

Epic 8 focused on establishing manual quality gates and advanced quality metrics. It successfully transitioned the project from automated-only QA to a hybrid model where Lead QAs can sample and approve AI work before production deployment.

- **Total Stories:** 9/9 Completed (100% completion)
- **Key Deliverables:** QA Sampling Queue, AI Code Review Summarizer, Production Approval Workflow, Quality Gate Configurator, Bug Escape Tracking, Anomaly Detection, and Workload Dashboards.

## 2. What Went Well (Successes)

- **Hybrid Human-AI Workflow:** The integration of AI-summarized diffs (8.2) into the human review process (8.UI) proved highly effective in reducing review time while maintaining high quality standards.
- **Deep Observability:** Comprehensive integration of Prometheus metrics and AI decision logging across all backend stories ensures high transparency and auditability.
- **UI Proactivity:** Following the "Visibility Gap" from Epic 7, the Lead QA dashboard (8.UI) was delivered alongside backend features, providing immediate value.
- **Predictive Quality:** The anomaly detection system (8.7) and bug escape tracking (8.5) establish a foundation for proactive quality management rather than reactive bug fixing.

## 3. Challenges & Critical Discoveries

- **Testing & Mocking Friction:** A recurring theme (carried over from Epic 17) is the friction in unit testing complex dependencies like Prisma and Prometheus. This led to significant testing debt in stories 8.6 and 8.8.
- **Implementation-Schema Mismatches:** Story 8.8 highlighted that the AI agent's internal model of the database schema (Prisma) sometimes drifts from reality (e.g., status enums `APPROVED`/`REJECTED`), leading to late-stage fixes.
- **Rollback Resilience:** The need to re-implement Story 8.4 due to a git rollback accidentally wiping the work indicates a need for more frequent pushes or state-check tools during development.
- **Integration Dependencies:** Late-stage integration of metrics (8.6) and anomaly detection (8.7) into other services proved complex and required several rounds of fixes.

## 4. Key Insights & Lessons Learned

- **"Implementation Readiness" is Mandatory:** We must verify that the AI has the _latest_ Prisma schema and API contracts before starting `EXECUTION` to prevent type mismatches.
- **Standardized Mocking Patterns:** We need a project-wide pattern for mocking Prometheus and Prisma to reduce the time spent on test setup.
- **UI-First Planning Works:** Embedding UI stories within the feature epic (8.UI) eliminated the directionless feel reported in Epic 7.

## 5. Impact on Future Epics (Epic 9 Preview)

Epic 9 will focus on **Error Classification & Handling**.

- **Lesson to Apply:** Use the anomaly detection patterns from 8.7 to automatically classify production errors.
- **Strategy:** Ensure the "Implementation Readiness" check includes a verification of the existing Error Logging infrastructure (Epic 11.2) before starting.
